# Organic Brand Post Designer

Design posts with a recognisable point of view, not merely a polished surface. The result should look art-directed for this brand, audience, message, and moment.

## Start with evidence

Before designing, inspect any supplied brand guide, logo, campaign history, product screenshots, photographs, and prior posts. In the ResortPro repository, read `FOUNDER_CONTEXT.md` and `.agents/social-media-context-sms.md`; verify claims in the product source when a visual contains factual copy.

If the brand has no usable visual system, infer a provisional one from the brief and label it as a proposed direction. Ask only for missing information that would materially change the design. Otherwise proceed with explicit assumptions.

For trend-led, platform-specific, or culturally sensitive work, research current references. Treat references as evidence of the visual landscape, not as templates to imitate. Verify current platform dimensions and safe zones at delivery time rather than relying on memorised numbers.

## Build the creative brief

Reduce the request to:

- audience and the real situation they are in;
- one message the post must communicate;
- desired response or one CTA;
- platform, format, language, and delivery size;
- proof and assets that may be used;
- brand traits to express and traits to avoid.

Write a one-sentence creative thesis in this form: “Show **specific truth** through **visual device** so **audience** feels or does **response**.” This is an internal decision tool, not mandatory client-facing copy.

If the user wants a full campaign or a new visual system, read [references/visual-system.md](references/visual-system.md). For a single post, use the compact method below.

## Choose a concept before styling

Develop 2–3 genuinely different concept territories when the direction is open. Each must differ in the idea, focal image, and composition—not just palette or font. Name each concept and state:

- the human or operational truth;
- the visual metaphor or documentary moment;
- the hierarchy and reading order;
- the brand anchors retained;
- the one deliberate surprise.

Choose the strongest direction using relevance, distinctiveness, brand fit, and production credibility. Do not average several concepts into a safe composite.

## Art-direct an organic visual

Use one dominant focal point and a clear first-second-third reading order. Let the caption carry explanations that do not need to be in the image.

Preserve a few stable brand anchors—such as color behavior, type behavior, grid, photo treatment, or a recurring motif—then vary one expressive dimension for the post. Recognition should come from the system, not from stamping the logo everywhere.

Prefer details that could only belong to this subject or audience:

- real environments, tools, gestures, materials, language, weather, and local context;
- documentary or observed moments over generic smiling-model scenes;
- crops with intention, natural expressions, believable lighting, and useful negative space;
- tactile details such as paper, ink, fabric, grain, or imperfect edges only when they fit the brand.

“Organic” does not mean random, messy, retro, or handmade by default. It means the design shows judgment, context, and a human point of view.

## Avoid the generic-AI look

Reject a direction when its main appeal comes from familiar model defaults: centered headline over a soft gradient, floating 3D objects, decorative blobs, gratuitous glassmorphism, neon purple-blue lighting, fake dashboards, hyper-symmetry, plastic skin, impossible reflections, meaningless microtext, or visual clutter disguised as richness.

Do not ban a technique when the brand genuinely owns it. The test is whether the choice is specific and intentional rather than statistically convenient.

When using image generation:

1. Generate the image or texture as an art-directed source asset when possible; add exact typography and logos in an editable layout afterward.
2. Describe subject, environment, camera/composition, light, material, emotional temperature, culturally specific details, negative space, and exclusions.
3. Avoid vague prompts such as “premium modern social media post.”
4. Inspect hands, faces, interfaces, signage, shadows, reflections, repeated objects, and cultural details at full size.
5. Never present invented people, properties, testimonials, screenshots, or results as real.
6. If text must be rendered inside a raster image, verify every character; regenerate or rebuild the text layer when it is wrong.

## Typography and layout

Use type as a voice, not decoration. Keep the family count restrained, but create contrast through scale, weight, width, rhythm, alignment, and spacing. Use no more hierarchy levels than the message needs.

Break a grid only for a reason: emphasis, pace, tension, or character. Preserve legibility at feed-thumbnail size. Avoid filling empty space merely because it exists.

For Bangla or bilingual work, verify that the chosen typeface supports the full script cleanly. Do not force awkward translations of familiar product terms. Check line breaks visually; Bangla and Latin text may need different optical sizing and spacing.

## Carousels

Design the carousel as a sequence rather than a stack of independent posters:

- cover: a specific tension, promise, or observation;
- middle: one beat per slide, with a visible rhythm and purposeful variation;
- final: resolution and one next action.

Keep stable anchors across slides while changing scale, crop, or composition to create momentum. Do not repeat the same centered card layout on every slide.

## Accessibility and truth

Aim for at least 4.5:1 contrast for normal text and 3:1 for large text. Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning. Keep important text and faces clear of platform UI and cropping zones.

Use only substantiated claims and licensed or user-supplied assets. Label mockups and conceptual imagery when confusion is possible. For sensitive contexts, preserve provenance and disclose meaningful generative edits when appropriate.

## Deliverables

Return the artifact the user requested, plus only the production details needed to use or revise it. For a visual design, include:

- final concept name and one-sentence rationale;
- exact on-art copy and CTA;
- dimensions/aspect ratio and safe-zone notes;
- palette and typography choices;
- asset or image-generation direction;
- accessibility/claim notes;
- export files or editable source when the available tools support them.

If only a prompt or brief was requested, do not generate a finished image without permission. If a finished visual was requested, iterate until it passes [references/quality-rubric.md](references/quality-rubric.md).

## Final review

Review at full size and as a small phone-feed thumbnail. Ask:

- Could this belong to five unrelated brands? If yes, add brand-specific evidence or remove a generic trope.
- Is the first thing seen also the most important thing?
- Does every element earn its place?
- Does the post contain a real point of view rather than only “nice styling”?
- Are the words, faces, hands, logos, product UI, and factual claims correct?
- Does the exported format remain readable after platform cropping and compression?

Use [references/quality-rubric.md](references/quality-rubric.md) for scored review or critique requests. The research behind these decisions is summarised in [references/research-basis.md](references/research-basis.md).
